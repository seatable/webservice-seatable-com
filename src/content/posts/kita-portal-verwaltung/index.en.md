## Why digital daycare management is becoming increasingly important

Cities, municipalities and independent providers face the demanding **task of ensuring reliable childcare** while coordinating numerous administrative processes at the same time. These include allocating childcare places, capacity and [staff planning]({{< relref "posts/personalplanung" >}}), billing parental fees and maintaining sensitive data.

In many facilities and municipalities, these **data and processes are spread across different systems**. Paper files, Excel spreadsheets, email inboxes and specialized administration programs exist side by side and make daycare management more difficult. If an address or phone number has changed, searching for up-to-date information costs valuable time.

Digital daycare management can make an important contribution here by **providing information centrally** and organizing kindergarten administration in a transparent way. The key is that the solution not only covers registration, but also supports the downstream tasks of daycare management.

## Benefits of digital daycare management

*   **More transparency**: All data and processes come together in one place, enabling structured collaboration between daycare centers, providers and parents.
*   **Less paperwork**: Digitalization eliminates most of the paper shuffling and ensures that important documents can always be found in the system.
*   **Time savings**: Automated work steps speed up everyday processes, leaving more time for what really matters – the educational work.
*   **Better planning**: A central overview of registrations, existing contracts and staff helps with capacity planning and compliance with the statutory staff-to-child ratios.
*   **Greater data security**: Modern solutions allow sensitive data to be stored securely, strict GDPR compliance and clear access and permission management.
    

## Daycare portal and place allocation: Centralizing registrations

Allocating childcare places is one of the biggest challenges in municipal daycare management. **Parents often register their children at several facilities** to improve their chances of getting a place. For individual daycare centers, this is not a problem – but for municipal needs planning it is. When several facilities have the same children on their waiting lists, it is difficult to determine the **actual demand**. At the same time, **available capacity** can go unnoticed because of the distorted figures, even though other daycare centers are overloaded.

![Daycare management: centralizing registrations and place allocation via a daycare portal](kita-verwaltung-platzvergabe.jpg)

**A central daycare portal creates a shared data foundation**. Registrations are recorded according to uniform criteria and can be matched against existing records. When allocating places, factors such as the child's date of birth, the guardians' address, the desired start date and other characteristics can be taken into account.

With modern [no-code solutions]({{< relref "posts/20250307-low-code" >}}), people without any technical background can create digital registration forms and flexible database solutions exactly the way they want. This makes it possible to build a daycare portal that reflects the specific needs of your municipality while still enabling a standardized process.

With clearly defined rules and consistent use of digital forms, **automatic data matching** can identify and clean up unwanted duplicate registrations. However, different spellings, incomplete information or several legitimate childcare requests may still require a manual review.

### What are the benefits of central registration via a daycare portal?

*   **Transparent place allocation**: Municipalities get a better overview of registrations, parents' priorities and available capacity at the daycare centers.  
*   **Detection of duplicate registrations**: Automated matching flags possible multiple registrations before they distort needs planning.
*   **Reliable waiting lists**: When new registrations come in or a registration status changes, waiting lists and occupancy figures update in real time.
*   **Better demand forecasts**: Consolidated data makes it easier to plan new groups and additional childcare services.
*   **Less manual work**: Uniform forms reduce follow-up questions and corrections, since the required information has to be entered in a specific format.
    

## Transparency for municipalities: Looking at capacity and childcare demand together

For cities and municipalities, it is not enough to know the situation at individual daycare centers. Municipal daycare management must be able to assess the **entire childcare demand across the municipality**. To do so, it needs to keep track of the number of registered children, the available places, planned capacity expansions and how demand is developing.

If the **data is spread across several systems and daycare providers**, getting an overall picture takes considerable effort. Statistics and reports for youth welfare offices, municipal committees or budget deliberations then often have to be painstakingly pieced together. On top of that, many daycare centers are not publicly run at all, so independent providers also have to be included in the planning.

{{< warning headline="Who runs daycare centers?" >}}

In Germany, two-thirds of all daycare centers are run by independent providers. These include churches and religious communities, non-profit associations and organizations (e.g. the Arbeiterwohlfahrt workers' welfare association) as well as private companies (e.g. for company daycare centers). Only one-third of daycare providers are cities and municipalities.

{{< /warning >}}

That is why only a **central daycare portal for municipal and independent providers** can serve as a shared data foundation. Nurseries, after-school care centers and elementary schools with all-day care can also be included, provided you design the data exchange with municipal daycare management accordingly. For cities and municipalities, this results in several benefits:

*   a uniform data foundation for needs planning
*   a better overview of available and occupied capacity
*   traceable trends in registrations and waiting lists
*   structured reports for municipal and political bodies
*   better coordination between different childcare services

Incidentally, an available place is not always suitable for every child: **Age, the desired amount of care and special needs** may go beyond the services or opening hours of certain daycare centers. In addition, the range of services should take into account that parents often have different preferences regarding **educational approaches** such as Montessori, Waldorf, inclusion, the situational approach, movement-focused or forest kindergartens. With solid figures, bottlenecks can be identified earlier and planning decisions can be made transparently that **take demand into account**.

## Strong master data management: Keeping all important information up to date

[Master data management]({{< relref "posts/20250505-master-data-management" >}}) begins with registration and is far from over once a place has been allocated. Throughout the entire term of the childcare contract, important child and family data must stay up to date – be it addresses, phone numbers, emergency contacts, pick-up authorizations, [health data]({{< relref "templates/healthcare/electronic-health-record" >}}) or other organizationally necessary details. Since providers also act as employers, staff master data is part of daycare management as well.  

Information on children's [allergies]({{< relref "templates/healthcare/allergy-diary" >}}), intolerances and chronic illnesses is particularly critical. Outdated phone numbers or files that cannot be found can lead to avoidable risks in an emergency. If the same information is kept in several places (in spreadsheets, programs or files), the risk of contradictory data also increases.

Central master data management bundles the information in a structured [relational database]({{< relref "posts/relationale-datenbank" >}}). There, you only need to make changes once to update the record in all linked tables at the same time. In addition, a digital system can allow parents or employees to update their own details via apps or forms.

### What data is needed for daycare administration?

*   **Personal data**: Names, dates of birth, addresses and relevant contact details of children, guardians or employees.
*   **Emergency contacts and pick-up authorizations**: Authorized persons for specific situations.
*   **Health-related information**: Important information on allergies, chronic illnesses and vaccinations.
*   **Childcare contracts**: Contract start date, amount of care and important agreements.
*   **Supporting documents**: Required documents from parents and employees, including, where applicable, birth certificates, proof of custody and identity, and certificates of good conduct.

One rule applies here: Not all sensitive data should be visible to all employees. A well-designed system for kindergarten administration should meet a high level of data protection and only provide the minimum data a person needs for their respective task.

## Staff planning and staff-to-child ratios: Realistically assessing workloads

Reliable childcare requires that enough qualified staff are available. Staff-to-child ratios are also required by law to ensure an **appropriate ratio of children per educator**. In many places, the calculated ratio for children under 3 is roughly 1:4 (1 educator for 4 children), and for children aged 3 and older up to school entry it is 1:10 (1 educator for 10 children).

![Daycare management: realistically assessing staff planning and staff-to-child ratios](kita-verwaltung-personalplanung.jpg)

In everyday daycare life, however, the ratio is often less favorable, since staff absences due to illness, [vacation]({{< relref "posts/20250324-urlaubsplanung" >}}) and [training]({{< relref "posts/workshop-planen" >}}), as well as working time for educational concepts, documentation and parent meetings, are not factored in. On top of that, waves of illness, part-time arrangements, covering longer opening hours and fluctuating numbers of children make scheduling even harder.

A **daycare duty roster** that is calculated too tightly can quickly reach its limits when several employees are absent at the same time. Managers then have to check at short notice which groups are affected, which colleagues can step in and whether care can still be guaranteed under the applicable requirements.

### Creating a daycare duty roster: What should you keep in mind?

Digital daycare management combines the relevant information into a shared overview. For staff planning, you can bring together the following data, for example:

*   planned [working hours]({{< relref "pages/landing-pages/use-cases/arbeitszeiterfassung" >}}) and availability
*   qualifications and possible assignments
*   absences and [sick notes]({{< relref "templates/human-resources/sick-leave-management" >}})
*   group sizes and care hours
*   applicable minimum staffing requirements

In a clearly structured database, daycare management can map out different scenarios, **plan in enough buffer** and document changes promptly. Automatic alerts help identify critical staffing levels before they cause problems in everyday operations. Especially during **waves of illness**, an up-to-date data foundation gives you an organizational advantage: You can see more quickly where action is needed and which alternatives are available.

When you create a daycare duty roster, you should consider staff availability, childcare demand and the applicable legal requirements together. The **staff-to-child ratios** are not the same in every federal state and for every age group. Missing qualifications and special operating rules can also lead to bottlenecks.

## From daycare portal to billing: Connecting administrative processes

Modern daycare management involves numerous tasks that go beyond registration, master data maintenance and staff planning. These include **contract management**, **parent communication** and **billing for daycare fees and meal costs**. If you handle these processes separately – for example, using Excel spreadsheets, paper files and specialized software in parallel – you have to enter information multiple times or transfer it manually. These scattered isolated solutions often lead to **data silos and media breaks** that cost time, create more administrative work and increase the risk of errors.

![Bring all data and processes together with a digital solution for daycare management](kita-verwaltung-no-code.jpg)

Your digital system for daycare administration should therefore cover all processes or **integrate seamlessly with other solutions**. Whether you can also automate a particular process depends on the data sources, the functional requirements and the available interfaces (e.g. for payment processing). A parent portal can also be built on top of internal daycare management. For direct access by guardians, you can set up **digital forms and apps** with the appropriate permissions.

## Data protection: Managing sensitive personal data securely

**Personal data of children, guardians and employees** is part of the everyday information that daycare centers must handle with particular care. Freely accessible lists, unprotected Excel files or shared user accounts make it difficult to reliably control access and track changes.

A daycare portal or digital system for daycare administration must therefore not only be practical, but also well thought out in terms of data protection law. For municipalities and providers, the following aspects are particularly relevant:

*   **Role and permission concept**: Daycare administration, daycare management and educators only receive the access rights they need for their tasks.  
*   **Structured data exchange**: Data is provided in a central database rather than being passed around haphazardly on slips of paper, by email or via text messages.
*   **Traceable changes**: Records, processing status and appointments can be updated in real time. Changes are documented in the history, and old versions can be restored.
*   **Data storage and data sovereignty**: Hosting, server locations and security measures play a major role in data protection, regardless of whether you opt for a [cloud solution]({{< relref "posts/cloud-computing" >}}) or your own [infrastructure]({{< relref "posts/it-infrastruktur" >}}).
*   **Data minimization**: You should only collect and process information that is necessary for the respective purpose. Data that is no longer needed must be deleted on time.

## How to get started with digital daycare management

SeaTable combines the structure and power of databases with the flexibility of no-code solutions. Because the possibilities are almost unlimited, however, a planned approach in six steps is recommended for successful digitalization. This approach makes the rollout easier, reduces unnecessary complexity and allows experience from everyday operations to feed directly into further development:

1. **Document existing processes**: Record how registrations, childcare contracts, master data and duty rosters have been managed so far.
2. **Define requirements**: Determine what a digital daycare portal, central master data management or staff planning should look like in the future.
3. **Responsibilities and access rights**: Specify which information you need, who maintains it and who is allowed to view it.
4. **Implement a pilot process**: Map a manageable process in SeaTable and test it with the responsible employees.
5. **Review data protection and rules**: Check whether permissions are set correctly, automations run reliably and whether there are duplicate records that you can replace with links.
6. **Expand step by step**: Only add further processes, employees or special cases once the basics are working.

## Frequently asked questions about digital daycare management

{{< faq "How does digital daycare management help maintain staff-to-child ratios?" >}}

Digital daycare management combines up-to-date information on staff working hours and absences with children's care hours. Automated alerts can draw attention to critical staffing levels. At the municipal level, a shared overview across all daycare centers helps align capacity, childcare demand and the applicable legal requirements. It is important to distinguish between calculated and actual compliance with staff-to-child ratios. In everyday daycare operations, the requirements can usually only be met through overstaffing and buffers in the duty roster.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "How can you prevent duplicate registrations when allocating daycare places?" >}}

Many parents register their children at several facilities to improve their chances of getting a childcare place. A central daycare portal enables municipalities to match incoming registrations against existing records in order to identify and clean up possible multiple registrations. The matches should be checked again so that legitimate requests are not mistakenly treated as duplicates.

{{< /faq >}}

{{< faq "How can you protect sensitive data and documents (e.g. vaccination records)?" >}}

Health-related information should only be accessible to authorized persons. Consistent role and permission management is therefore essential to restrict access for unauthorized persons. For GDPR compliance, secure storage, restrictions on sharing and timely deletion of data are also essential. With cloud solutions for daycare management, security measures and data center locations are also relevant, for example, so that you can keep track of data flows and data processing. However, you can only achieve full data sovereignty with a self-hosted solution.

Cross-facility [data management]({{< relref "posts/20250317-datenmanagement" >}}) also makes municipal needs planning across different types of childcare easier.
