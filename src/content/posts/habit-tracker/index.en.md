Making **good resolutions** for the new year has become a habit for many people. But every year, people are disappointed when they once again fail to follow through on their resolutions. It simply takes a strong will to integrate new habits into our lives. **Habit trackers** exist to make this process easier.

They keep motivation high and also spark the ambition to maintain good behaviors or break a bad habit. In this article, you will find out how a habit tracker works and what you need to know to create a habit tracker that suits your needs.

## What can a habit tracker do?

Building new habits and changing bad ones – a habit tracker can help you with this. In its most basic form, it is a **list with checkboxes** in which you document every day over a period of one month whether or not you have carried out an action. Have you resolved to meditate every day? Then put a tick in the box whenever you have actually kept your resolution on that day. If you maintain your habit tracker regularly, you will get a picture that shows your **progress**.

There are no limits to your imagination when **designing** your habit tracker. For example, work with different **colors** and fonts or simply implement your ideas online with the habit tracker from SeaTable. You should write down not only the habits you are aiming for, but also your current ones. Tracking helps you become aware of the habits that you have taken for granted up to this point.

But remember: Your habit tracker is not made to last forever. The aim is to develop a **routine** so that at some point you no longer need to record your activity.

## Why it helps to record your progress

In addition to the goal of learning habits and integrating them into your everyday life, tracking has some useful side effects. For example, you benefit from being able to see your **successes** as well as your **setbacks** without distortion. This way, you don't give excuses a chance and can **reflect on** your results.

In addition, the quickly recognizable successes boost your self-confidence and encourage you to continue in a **motivated** and **disciplined** way. With a habit tracker, you quickly develop the ambition to keep your winning streak going.

{{< warning headline="Tracking helps you achieve your goals" text="Science also shows that tracking helps you achieve your goals. Studies show that people who track their progress are more successful in achieving their goals." />}}

Probably the biggest advantage is **building routines**. By becoming aware of your progress, you can internalize your habits. The habit tracker itself is the best proof of this. After all, regularly ticking off your [to-dos]({{< relref "posts/to-do-liste-online" >}}) is also a habit.

## Which habits you can track

Whether it's little things in everyday life or turning your whole life upside down – basically anything is possible. Of course, they should be habits that you consider **worthwhile** and that help you grow. Or simply something that you enjoy. The rule is: if you want to aim high, you have to start small. You can always step it up later.

Think about habits that will **improve your everyday life** and can be integrated into it. Also become aware of which habits you already have and whether they need changing. Whether you want to break bad habits or learn new ones, you will find inspiration here:

## Changing habits

Many **undesirable patterns of behavior** have become established in our everyday lives and have quietly turned into habits. Getting rid of them or, ideally, turning them into good habits takes a lot of time and energy. But with a **plan**, you can succeed.

![6 steps lead to success in changing your habits.](Purple-Colorful-Modern-Roadmap-Timeline-Infographic-1-e1706188296694-711x1538.jpg)

This plan will help you achieve your goals.

### Set a goal

All beginnings are difficult. The first question you need to ask yourself is what your **goal** should be. What do you want to achieve by changing your habits? This question is important to clarify your **motivation**. Now think about what you want to record with your habit tracker. To start with, you should track no more than **five habits**, two of which are new. In terms of time, it makes sense to focus on **one month** at first.

Don't take on too much, because a quick setback could lead you to drop all your good intentions again. You can also make tracking easier if you set a specific time of day to write things down or tick everything off right after the activities.

### Create commitment

Don't keep your plans to yourself. By telling your friends or relatives about your goals, you create additional **commitment**. You are no longer only accountable to yourself, but also want to be able to tell the people you trust about your successes.

### Establish a routine

Carrying out your behaviors regularly requires strong **self-discipline**. However, there are tips to help you integrate habits into your everyday life more quickly.

![The Atomic Habits book contains the tips below, which you can integrate into your habit tracker.](jose-mizrahi-YaM1l94EJwc-unsplash-min-scaled-e1701350426432-711x682.jpg)

The following tips are from James Clear's book **Atomic Habits** and can make your habit tracker even more effective.

{{< accordion "Build habits into your everyday life" >}}

To make a desirable action more binding, you should link it to the place and time at which you perform it.
{{< /accordion >}}

{{< accordion "Combine new and old" >}}

Habits that are already automated can make it easier for you to learn new habits. Simply perform a current habit and a new habit one after the other.
{{< /accordion >}}

{{< accordion "Use incentives and rewards" >}}

Not all habits that you want to learn will be enjoyable. It can therefore help to work with a reward system. Perform a task that you don't like and then reward yourself with something that you find easy and fun.
{{< /accordion >}}

{{< accordion "2-minute habits" >}}

If an activity takes less than two minutes, you can do it immediately, as it requires little energy and willpower. At the beginning of your tracking journey, it makes sense to start small. Habits that you can do in two minutes are motivating and offer the opportunity to expand them as you wish.

{{< /accordion >}}

### Celebrate progress

The longer you stick to a habit, the easier it will be for you to carry it out. It usually takes around two to three months for a new habit to become **automatic**. But it's a long journey and you should duly celebrate every milestone along the way. To make it easier for you to achieve your goals, you can add **mini-habits** to your habit tracker. These are stages on your way to your goal.

For example, suppose you resolve to read 30 pages every day. If you don't manage this, however, it's no reason to give up completely. Start by reading five pages a day and try to **increase** this slowly. Set yourself a minimum that you definitely want to achieve in a day – then you can be happy about everything you achieve beyond that.

### Overcome setbacks

Anyone who makes progress will also have to cope with setbacks. Not carrying out your habits once is not the end of the world. It can easily happen if you simply wanted to take on too many good resolutions and they are now overwhelming you. Take the time to reflect on **why** you were unable to achieve your goals.

Are there too many habits? Then adjust them or **reduce the workload**. Don't like an activity after all? That can happen too. You won't always like a habit as much as you imagined, and that's okay. Once you have realized the reason for your stumble, it's important to continue with a revised habit tracker. Because if you give up, all your efforts will have been in vain. So remind yourself why you want to change your habits and find a new **source of motivation** to [overcome procrastination]({{< relref "posts/prokrastination" >}}).

### Automate habits

And you've reached your goal! You have consistently maintained your habit tracker and the habits have become firmly integrated into your everyday life. That's great – but achieving goals takes time. According to the latest [studies](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ejsp.674), it takes an average of **66 days** to build a habit. However, what matters is not so much how long a behavior is performed, but how often.

## Create a habit tracker

As already mentioned, there are no limits to the design of your habit tracker. Creating it individually by hand can help to create additional commitment through personalization and provide extra motivation thanks to the work you have already invested.

![The template shows a more creative approach, working with colors and a special shape.](Ivory-Colorful-Bullet-Journal-Habit-Tracker-Page-A5-Document-1-e1706188603736-711x944.jpg)

You can design your habit tracker by hand or with templates. If you like it practical and simple, you can also use an **app** or **online template**. These are usually less creative, but bundle all habits in a clear and structured way. Compared to notebooks and paper calendars, **digital habit trackers** can be flexibly adapted if your habits change.
