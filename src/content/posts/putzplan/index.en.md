Anyone who lives with several people under one roof knows the situation: dirty dishes are piling up in the sink, the shower drain is clogged, and someone really should vacuum again. But no one feels responsible and the annoying work remains undone. To put an end to the chaos, an individual cleaning schedule for your household is the solution. Here you can find out everything you need to know to create your cleaning schedule.

## Why a cleaning schedule is worthwhile

When many people live in a household, disagreements inevitably arise. This is also true when it comes to **cleanliness**. Not everyone thinks that they have to wash their dishes immediately or that regular cleaning in the bathroom is necessary.

![Dirty dishes accumulate quickly when there is no cleaning schedule and no one feels responsible.](nathan-dumlao-m0ucB-06v7k-unsplash-1-e1693817126757-711x533.jpg)

Dirty dishes accumulate quickly when there is disagreement about cleanliness.

Therefore, it helps to sit down together and create a joint plan that everyone is happy with in the end. If everyone pulls together, the tasks are done quickly and you immediately feel more comfortable in your own four walls. The cleaning schedule assigns the tasks in a binding way, but of course it does not apply for all eternity. If someone is not satisfied, you should adjust the plan and develop it further.

## What needs to be done in which room?

To get a rough overview of the tasks that need to be done, it is helpful to divide them up by room. Basic tasks such as vacuuming, cleaning the floor, taking out the trash, dusting the cupboards, or tidying up come up in all rooms. For specific tasks in the individual rooms, a possible cleaning schedule might look like this:

- **Bathroom:** clean the toilet, wipe the mirror, descale the faucet, squeegee the shower, air out properly
- **Kitchen:** keep the countertop clean, dispose of leftovers, wash the dishes, clean the sink, clean the oven, defrost and clean the refrigerator and freezer
- **Living room:** wipe the TV, shake out the cushions, fold the blankets
- **Hallway:** tidy up the dresser, keep the coat rack neat, clean the mirror
- **Bedroom:** make the bed, [tidy up the closet]({{< relref "posts/kleiderschrank-organisieren" >}}), do the laundry (everyone usually takes care of this themselves)

{{< warning headline="Good to know: Where the annoying dust comes from" >}}

Recurring house dust can drive some people crazy or even trigger [allergies]({{< relref "templates/healthcare/allergy-diary" >}}). It consists of tiny particles that come from different sources. Small particles (e.g. pollen, sand, soot) enter the home when airing, dirt falls off shoes, clothing and upholstery lose microscopic fibers, people and pets shed skin flakes and hair, crumbs fall on the floor, and insects such as mites leave behind excretions and decompose into dust themselves after their death. So, unfortunately, the formation of house dust cannot be avoided, but you can reduce it with a cleaning schedule.

## Distribute tasks fairly

Decide together who will take on which tasks. Take into account the daily routines of all residents. How much time can you devote to household cleanliness each day? There are different approaches to distributing tasks: Ask about each person's **preferences**. Some like to take care of the dishes, while others don't mind taking out the trash. In the best case, all tasks are already distributed this way.

If this is not the case, another option is to **rotate** the tasks. In this case, responsibilities change on a weekly basis. This is usually particularly effective, since the workload is roughly the same for everyone after one full rotation and everyone has already been responsible for everything once. However, it takes longer for a certain routine to settle into everyday life.

![Teamwork is the key to a successful cleaning schedule.](pexels-pavel-danilyuk-8763139-1-scaled-e1693817422843-711x498.jpg)

Teamwork is the key to a successful cleaning schedule.

In order to convince even [cleaning slackers](https://www.stuttgarter-zeitung.de/inhalt.putztipps-fuer-faule-mhsd.c745ba0a-445a-4ced-962a-27efccd135af.html) of your plan, a **creative solution** for the distribution of tasks is the key to success. For example, you can draw lots each week to decide who takes on which task. Or you can develop a point system in which each completed task earns points and each of you has to reach a minimum number of points. This is fun and promotes a good atmosphere.

## Stay motivated

The tasks are distributed, but no one does what they should? Then perhaps it's due to a **lack of motivation**. No wonder, because cleaning is not exactly one of many people's favorite activities. To keep the cleaning schedule going anyway, it can help to find the right incentive and to [overcome procrastination]({{< relref "posts/prokrastination" >}}).

![Having a little fun while cleaning makes it much easier to get the tasks done.](pexels-rdne-stock-project-5591970-1-711x474.jpg)

Combine the tedious cleaning with listening to music or watching series. With a little fun while cleaning, everything immediately becomes much easier.

But make sure you don't get too distracted – cleaning should still be your main focus. Or take advantage of your roommates' presence and do particularly strenuous tasks together. Another option is to take a playful approach to the whole thing. For example, you can introduce rewards or penalties to spur your roommates on to top performance.
