## Definition: What is expense reimbursement?

The German [Income Tax Act](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/) defines expense reimbursement as a situation in which an employee incurs expenses in the name and for the account of the employer (advance payment) and is reimbursed for them one-to-one by the company. Under § 3 No. 50 EStG, it is exempt from tax and social security contributions. The exact process and the accepted expense categories can be regulated both by law and internally within the company.

## Types of expenses you should know

Sections 4 and 5 of the German Income Tax Act contain the relevant regulations regarding business expenses. For example, you can have costs incurred in connection with **business trips** reimbursed as expenses. The same applies to expenses arising from the fulfillment of orders or the provision of services, such as a **business meal** with your customers. You can also claim **purchases** or orders such as office supplies or fruit for the office kitchen as reimbursable expenses.

## Expense reimbursement vs. transitory funds

Have you already looked into expense reimbursement and come across the term **transitory funds**? Then you have probably wondered what exactly the difference is. In principle, the same regulations apply to both expense reimbursement and transitory funds. The only difference is the **point in time at which the employee receives the money**:

- **Expense reimbursement**: You pay in advance and are only reimbursed after the transaction.
- **Transitory funds**: You do not have to pay out of your own pocket in advance, but receive the funds from your company before you spend them.

Transitory funds are generally used for **foreseeable expenses** that are incurred at regular intervals, whereas expense reimbursements tend to be used for **one-off expenses**.

## Exception: Flat-rate expense reimbursement

Companies can also settle the reimbursement of expenses with a **flat rate**. These flat-rate amounts can include, for example, **travel, meal and accommodation costs**. However, you must bear in mind that the tax office sets the maximum rates. The annual maximum for accommodation and meals is 1,000 euros. If you exceed this, an itemized expense reimbursement makes more sense than a flat-rate one.

## Conditions for expenses exempt from tax and social security contributions

According to [§ 3 No. 50 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__3.html), expenses are **exempt from tax and social security contributions**. To ensure that the tax office recognizes the expenses as tax-free, you must meet a few **conditions**. Here is an overview of the most important ones:

- **Earmarking**: Only expenses that you incur in the name and at the expense of your company count as reimbursable expenses.
- **Documentation**: You must prove your expenses with legally valid, original invoices and receipts. For amounts up to 250 euros, a simple receipt is sufficient. According to the current [GoBD guidelines](https://ao.bundesfinanzministerium.de/ao/2021/Anhaenge/BMF-Schreiben-und-gleichlautende-Laendererlasse/Anhang-64/anhang-64.html), scanning receipts is expressly permitted.
- **Work equipment**: The expenses must be clearly necessary for business purposes and must not include any private items. If the evidence is unclear, the tax office assumes a benefit in kind.
- **Correct settlement**: Expenses should be settled properly, with each individual item shown separately on the receipt or invoice.
- **Flat-rate expense reimbursement**: You must keep traceable records of regular expenses, as the tax office only grants a flat rate if documentation is available for a certain period of time.

## Challenges you need to master in expense reimbursement

Expense reimbursement comes with its own challenges. In particular, the **bureaucratic effort** should not be underestimated – receipts and documentation pile up in folders and take up **space**. To be able to settle everything in full, every receipt and every invoice must be meticulously submitted. However, there is a risk that the proof gets lost faster than you would like.

{{< warning headline="Tip" text="If you do lose a receipt, you can create a **self-issued receipt**. Before doing so, however, it is advisable to consult your local tax office, as there is no general obligation to accept it." />}}

The expense reimbursement process is not only **time-consuming**, but also **error-prone**, especially if it is still done on paper. You need to be fully focused so as not to accidentally transpose any digits. In addition, there are the tax office's **format requirements** to consider, as it only accepts combined PDFs.

Companies and employees must show **perseverance** and **precision** during expense reimbursement in order to master the challenge successfully. However, there is no need to worry, because even in the biggest jumble of receipts and forms, there is always a silver lining.

The annoying **paper chaos** that often accompanies traditional methods is completely eliminated.
